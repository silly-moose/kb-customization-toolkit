A guided path is a normal article. You write steps as headings, text, and bullets, and the knowledge base turns them into a step-by-step view. There's no code, and a mistake can't break anything: readers get a normal article, and authors get a Path check that says what to fix.

Write a path in five moves

  1. Start from the template. Copy the Guided path template. The first paragraph becomes the path's summary. A "Before you start:" line followed by a bulleted list becomes a checklist box.
  2. Write your steps. Each Heading 2 is one step. Start it with a type word and a colon (Action:, Decision:, or Judgement:), then the step's name. Everything under the heading belongs to that step.
  3. Give decisions their options. Under a Decision: heading, add a bulleted list with one bullet per option: the option's text, an arrow, then the name of the step it leads to, like "Wrong password → Reset the password". Typing -> works too.
  4. Add special lines where you need them. Each is a paragraph that starts with a word and a colon:
    • Confirm: a tick-box the reader must check before continuing.
    • End: ends this branch. The rest of the line is the completion message.
    • Escalate: when to stop and hand off. Shown as a note in the step.
    • Go to: where this step continues, by step name. Use it when a branch rejoins a later step.
  5. View the article. An article with at least two step headings becomes a path automatically. Authors see the Path check bar at the top of the article page.

Examples to open in the editor: A customer can't sign in (for agents on a call), Why can't I see an article? (for readers finding their own answer), and Example with a mistake (what the Path check shows when something is wrong).

Step types

You typeReaders get
Action: Reset the passwordSomething to do, then Next. Also the default when a heading has no type word.
Decision: What happens when they sign in?A choice: the reader picks an option and jumps to that step.
Judgement: Locked accountA step that needs the reader's judgment, usually with an Escalate: line.

When branches rejoin

By default, Next goes to the step written below. When two branches lead to the same later step, each branch above the last one needs a Go to: line naming that step. Otherwise Next walks the reader into the other branch. The Path check marks these steps with "continues at step N".

What the Path check tells you

  • Green: every step, option, and ending checks out.
  • Amber: the path works, but something needs a look, such as a step nothing leads to.
  • Red: the path is off, for example because an option names a step that doesn't exist. Readers see a normal article, and the panel names each problem.

The Path check appears on the article page (preview or live), not in the editor.

Editing a live path

  • Changing wording or images is always safe.
  • Renaming a step: update the options and Go to: lines that name it. Its usage history starts over.
  • Moving steps: check where Next goes afterwards.

Step types and special lines also work in German (Aktion, Entscheidung, Ermessen, Bestätigen, Ende, Eskalieren, Weiter zu) and Spanish (Acción, Decisión, Criterio, Confirmar, Fin, Escalar, Ir a).
